Duskvale

Build the sound of your own night

Not a finished scene — a mixer

Most ambient apps hand you a finished recording: “Rain in the forest”, “Fireplace”. Duskvale hands you the layers instead. Start the rain, slide a distant wind underneath it, keep the fire low — and you end up with a night nobody else has.

There are six families of layers, 28 recordings in all. Each family has its own volume slider, and you can run as many of them at once as you like.

How it works

  • Pick a layerTap one of the numbers on the Rain row. The sound fades up gently — nothing starts abruptly.
  • Stack more on topPick a recording from the other families too. One recording plays per family, and the families are independent of each other.
  • Find the balanceEach family's slider pushes that layer forward or back. Tapping another number in the same family crossfades between the two, so there is no gap.
  • Watch it, if you wantFlip the switch at the top to Color, pick a few colours off the wheel and tap Visualize: a full-screen field where those colours drift and blend.

The sound catalogue

  • Rain 8 recordingsFrom drizzle to a downpour on glass
  • Wind 4 recordingsDistant howl and passing breeze
  • Water 4 recordingsStream, waves, dripping
  • Fire 4 recordingsCrackling wood and embers
  • Night Nature 4 recordingsCrickets and night birds
  • Resonance 4 recordingsLow, sustained tones

Every recording ships inside the app. Nothing is streamed: it works the same on a plane, on the underground or in airplane mode, and it spends none of your mobile data.

The loop points were trimmed by hand, so a recording starting over gives you no click and no gap. Sound fades in over 0.7 seconds and out over 0.9.

The colour side

A colour panel sits on the same screen as the mixer. Drag your finger across the wheel to pick a colour and add it to your list; tap Visualize and those colours become a full-screen fluid field. The blobs do not stay round — they flow into each other, and the pattern never repeats.

If your phone has “reduce motion” turned on, the visualiser holds still: it draws a single frame rather than leaving the screen blank.

No account, no sign-up

Duskvale opens and works. It asks for no e-mail, makes you register nothing, and sends nothing to a server of ours. The only thing it keeps on your phone is the interface language you picked; your colour list lives only for that session.

Deleting the app deletes everything the app has — there is no account left behind.
